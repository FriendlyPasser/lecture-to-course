Follow-up appointments among morning appointments

Author-created cumulative review · New setting and numbers.

A clinic recorded 120 appointments. Thirty were in the morning, and 9 of those morning appointments were follow-ups. Select one morning appointment uniformly at random. What is the probability that it was a follow-up? Enter the exact probability.

Hint: identify the group selected from

Which appointments could be selected under the stated condition? Use the size of that eligible group, rather than every appointment recorded, as the denominator.

Let F mean follow-up and M mean morning. There are 30 eligible morning appointments and 9 satisfy both conditions, so P(F | M) = (9/120)/(30/120) = 9/30 = 0.3 = 30%, exactly. P(M) = 30/120 is positive; uniform selection justifies the count ratio. No independence assumption is needed.

Dividing 9 by 120 gives the joint share of appointments that were both morning and follow-up. Dividing 30 by 120 gives the morning share. Neither gives the follow-up share within morning appointments.
