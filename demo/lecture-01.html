• Identify the reference group in a probability question.
  • Calculate a conditional probability and distinguish it from a joint probability.

01 / THE IDEA

The denominator tells the story.

A survey records 100 students. Forty take statistics; 24 of those also take economics. We want the fraction taking economics among statistics students.

100All students
→
40Take statistics
→
24Also take economics

The phrase “among statistics students” sets the . We count 24 successes out of 40 eligible students, not out of all 100.

02 / WORKED EXAMPLE

Condition first. Then count.

measures the share of an event inside a specified group. Let E mean economics and S mean statistics.

P(E|S)=P(E∩S)P(S)

Divide the probability of both events by the probability of the conditioning event. This requires P(S) > 0.

Show the three calculation steps
  1. The joint share is 24 / 100 = 0.24.
  2. The conditioning group has share 40 / 100 = 0.40.
  3. Divide: 0.24 / 0.40 = 24 / 40 = 0.60.

24%

Supplementary explanation

Think of conditioning as filtering a list before computing a percentage. It does not mean statistics causes students to choose economics.

03 / CHECK YOUR REASONING

Which group are you counting?

What is P(E | S) in this survey?

Conditioning on S limits the group to 40 statistics students. Of those, 24 take economics. The first choice is the joint probability; the third is the probability of statistics alone.

Check the source · p. 2

Common mistake: seeing the same numerator and assuming the probabilities mean the same thing. Always name the denominator's group.
