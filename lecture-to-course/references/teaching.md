# Teaching from lecture PDFs

Write for a student taking this subject, not a generic beginner. Infer level from the material. Explain unfamiliar terms when introduced without replacing the course with a beginner overview.

For each source page record: topic, reliable text/formulas, visual evidence needed, uncertainties, and destination chapter (or an explicit reason for omission, e.g. duplicate title slide). Maintain this coverage map in the working folder, not necessarily the student website.

Start a lecture with what the learner should be able to do. Teach concepts in source order unless a small regrouping clearly helps and does not break dependencies. Introduce a formula's variables, units and assumptions before using it. Reveal worked steps without withholding essential explanations behind decorative interaction.

Distinguish three things: source content (cited), supplementary explanation (labeled), and unclear source content (not resolved by guessing). Do not fabricate instructor intent, exam likelihood, theorem conditions or missing numerical values. A correct new example is still supplementary.

Quizzes should test applying a supported idea: choosing a method, recognizing a misconception, interpreting a diagram, or completing a step. Explain why the correct answer follows and why plausible alternatives fail. Validate calculations independently. Cite the relevant source pages. Do not use a quiz solely to satisfy a quota on a title-only lecture.

Prefer diagrams for relationships and processes, tables for comparisons, and prose for reasoning. There is no visual-percentage requirement. Preserve necessary depth even when that takes more space. No compulsory chat animation or metaphor.

Glossary entries use the English term as the main label, a conventional Simplified Chinese equivalent, and a short English definition consistent with the lecture. Include specialized abbreviations and symbols when helpful. Avoid confident Chinese translations when terminology is ambiguous; retain alternatives with context.
